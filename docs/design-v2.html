REDESIGN · DRAFT FOR DISCUSSION
Moscow Rules · design board · after playtest 1

Stratego, with the reports doing the talking

Playtest 1 felt like chess with a typing tax. Version 2 removes the typing and makes the board readable. Most of a week goes on gathering intelligence. Attacks and traps become rare, slow, visible plays that each side can see coming, if it is paying attention.

Decided
  • No typing: click an agent, pick an order
  • Everyone's position is visible; roles and loyalty are hidden
  • Claude writes the reports, for both sides
  • Five orders a week; any city is one week away
  • Anyone but a Talent-spotter can kill
  • Attacks are prepared in the open; traps counter them
  • Recruiting takes three approaches, with no per-game cap
  • Fixed rosters: no new agents
  • Karla is an opponent who makes moves
Dropped from v1
  • Free-text cables and the cable interpreter
  • Codes and codebooks
  • The rock-paper-scissors combat cycle
  • Three recruit attempts per game
  • Watch as the only way to see anyone
Still open
  • Who can set traps
  • Exact cover costs
  • Scoring and how to win
  • Retiring your own suspected moles
The loop

One week, three beats

The board

See everyone. Know no one.

As in Stratego, you can see every piece and you can't see what any of them are. The map shows every agent in every city, yours and theirs, by name and cover. Whether they are a Scalphunter or a Ferret, and whether they are still loyal, is what the reports help you work out.

Blue: your agents, roles known to you. Red: theirs, with cover identity and any role you've confirmed. A hollow dot means an agent is on their way in. Moves happen in plain sight.

Player roles

Four specialists, each with its own way of knowing

The combat cycle is gone. Anyone except a Talent-spotter can kill, and whether a strike succeeds depends on information (is a trap waiting?), not on rank. Each role adds a different way of finding things out.

Scalphunter

Power
Interrogates. Whoever it takes down gives up their role and the name of anyone they were handling on your side.
Strike
Can strike with no preparation, so harder to see coming.
Risk
Walks into traps like anyone else.

Lamplighter

Power
Surveillance. Learns the roles of everyone in its city. Reliably spots strikes being prepared and traps being set.
Strike
Needs a week of preparation.
Risk
Surveillance wears down its own cover.

Ferret

Power
Counter-intelligence. Notices anyone surveilling or approaching your people in its city. Can vet one of your own agents for loyalty.
Trap
Sets traps faster than anyone (open question).
Risk
Vetting takes the week.

Talent-spotter

Power
Recruits. Three approaches over separate weeks turn an enemy agent.
Strike
Cannot kill.
Risk
Every approach might be reported to the other side.

Each side has 8 agents, 2 per role, and no reinforcements. Icons appear with role names only where a role is explained; on the map, roles show as small codes.

Mostly intelligence, occasionally violence

Attacks and traps

The play and the counter-play

A strike needs a week of preparation in the target's city, and preparation can be seen. That gives each side a turn to read the other. Step through it:

Control orders Hale to prepare a strike on Novak in Vienna. Karla's people in Vienna may notice someone studying Novak's routine.

How a strike resolves

No trapTrap set
StrikeTarget diesAttacker dies
Call offNothing happensTrap stays; it took a week to set

Why put a plan in motion at all

Strikes are the only way to win, and preparation is the only way to strike, apart from Scalphunters. Holding a prepared strike wears the attacker's cover down each week. Waiting has a price, and so does a trap set against an attack that never comes.

Recruitment

A slow seduction, not a coin flip

There's no per-game limit, so Talent-spotters matter all game. Progress is never lost, but an exposed approach tells the other side who your Talent-spotter is and whom they're working on. A turned agent stays on the other side's board and still takes their orders, but reports to you: their orders reach you, their reports home are lies, and their strikes fail.

The reports

Where the game is actually played

Every agent reports every week. The engine decides what each agent could have seen, from their role, their city and their cover, and Claude writes it up. Claude can't add facts. Agents report on the enemy, on events, and on each other.

Open questions

Still to decide

  • Who can set traps?Ferrets only keeps the roles distinct but makes traps scarce (two per side). Anyone except Talent-spotters, with Ferrets faster, makes traps common enough to fear.
  • How hard each action is on cover.The pips above are a first guess. The balance simulation will tune them, aiming for most agents to reach "thin" once or twice a game, and for "blown" to be rare and costly.
  • Scoring and how to win.Keep "most eliminations after 12 weeks, or wipe the other side out"? A turned agent could count toward the side that turned it, as now.
  • Retiring your own suspected moles.From playtest 1. With Ferret vetting, is a separate "kill your own" order still needed? If so: does killing a loyal agent count as a kill for Karla?
  • Can the side that turned an agent direct its lies?Choose from a few lies by clicking (hide a trap, frame a loyal agent, call a city empty), or let the engine choose plausible ones?
