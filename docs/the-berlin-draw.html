The chase

A pitch has to be made face to face

Turning an agent takes two evenings, in two different weeks, in the same city. Every position is public, so Control could watch Karla's Talent-spotter follow Fenwick across Europe.

Fenwick started in Rome, struck from Tangier, held Berlin, fled to Tangier in week 8 and came back in week 10.

Novak arrived in Berlin in week 2 and followed him both times, a week behind.

Control knew, because Molnar told him: "Novak, their Talent-spotter, was ordered to approach Fenwick."

The decisive exchange

One lie, through one mole

The game was settled by a single sentence Control put in Molnar's mouth three weeks before the end.

  1. Week 9 · Control plants

    Molnar reports home: "I don't like what I'm seeing from Tamas. Meetings I can't account for. I think they've been turned."

  2. Week 12 · Karla acts

    "The score only adds up if Tamas is London's." Karla executes his own Ferret, loyal all along.

  3. The last night · the cost

    No trap over Berlin. Fenwick kills Novak before the second pitch lands. Two each.

The game

Moscow Rules

Your best agent may already be theirs.

  • A spy duel on a map of Cold War Europe, in the spirit of le Carré. You play Control; Claude plays Karla, and writes down why.
  • Every agent's position is public. Their roles, and their loyalty, are hidden until you dig them out.
  • Turn their agents, feed fake orders to your suspected moles, frame their loyal people, and read the morning traffic for the lie.
  • Twelve weeks, half an hour. The side with the most agents still working for it wins.
From a real game of Moscow Rules, played November 2026. Orders, reasons and journal entries are quoted from the game's own record.
