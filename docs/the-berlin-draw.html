MOSCOW RULESa spy game · built with Claude

A spy game where the AI across the table is lying to you.

You run British intelligence. Claude runs the KGB. For twelve weeks you both recruit each other's agents, feed each other false reports, and try to work out who on your own side has quietly switched.

On the human's screen
"Another evening with Molnar. 1 of 2: they're listening."
My recruiter's report. One more meeting and Molnar, one of the AI's assassins, would be working for me.
In Claude's private notes, the same night
"Fenwick has shown his hand on Molnar, so Molnar goes to ground."
The AI hid Molnar from my assassin to keep him safe.

That night the second meeting landed. Claude was busy protecting an agent who was switching sides as it wrote. That's the game.

How it plays

Chess, if any piece could secretly be playing for the other side

Six to ten agents each, on a map of Cold War Europe. Thirty minutes a game.

You see everyone. You know no one.

Every agent's location is public. What they do, and whose side they're really on, is hidden.

Turn their agents.

Two quiet meetings and an enemy agent works for you. They keep their post, leak every order they're given, and send their own side whatever lies you choose.

Feed lies. Test loyalty.

Give a suspect a fake order and watch whether the enemy reacts. Frame their loyal people and let them do the rest.

An opponent that thinks out loud.

Claude plays the other side with the same fog of war you have, and writes down why it made every move. You read its diary when the game ends.

One real game

Both sides of the table, from a game I just played

Six agents each. The quotes on the right are Claude's own notes, written as it played and hidden from me until the end.

On my screen

Claude had turned the one agent I relied on to protect everyone else, and used him to keep reporting that all was well.

Claude executed its own loyal agent on my lie. That left Berlin unguarded on the last night.

Wk 12The last night

Its greed, my knife, a draw

On my screen
"Novak is dead in Berlin."

"Novak makes the second pitch on Fenwick rather than Kral putting him down, because a turned man counts twice."

One more meeting and Claude would have taken my last agent and the game. It reached for the bigger prize, and with no trap over Berlin my assassin got there first. Two agents each. A draw on the final night.

Why it's different

The opponent isn't following a script

Most game AI is a set of rules tuned to look clever. Here the AI reads the same reports you do and makes judgment calls about people.

It can be fooled

Claude acts on what it believes. Plant the right lie and it will execute its own agent. It can also be right about you.

It reasons like a spymaster

It weighs whether a man is worth more dead or turned, which of its agents might be compromised, and when a report is bait.

Nothing plays out twice

Different cities, different agents, different lies. At the end, an analyst writes up the game: the turning point, who was fooled, and where each side went wrong.
